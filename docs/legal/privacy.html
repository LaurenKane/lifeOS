Privacy Notice — LifeOS

Personal, self-hosted financial ledger · single user · not a commercial service

Who is responsible

LifeOS is a personal application operated by a single individual for their own use. The individual is the sole data controller. This notice describes what the software does with data; it is not a corporate privacy policy and LifeOS is not a company.

What data is processed

Only financial data the account holder has explicitly chosen to import:

  • Bank and credit-card transaction records (date, amount, currency, description, category)
  • The original bank export files from which those records were parsed (CSV, CAMT, PDF)
  • Account metadata (name, type, IBAN or masked card number, institution)
  • User-created labels: categories, merchants, and categorization rules

LifeOS does not collect, transmit, or hold behavioural data, telemetry, analytics, advertising identifiers, or device identifiers. The software makes no third-party requests except to the bank data provider the account holder explicitly configures.

Where data is stored

Entirely on infrastructure chosen and controlled by the account holder — typically a private server, and a private database on it. LifeOS has no central service, no user accounts, and no server-side copy of any kind. There is nothing to be breached on LifeOS's side, because there is no LifeOS side.

The database is not encrypted at the application layer. Protection at rest relies on the storage encryption of the host, which is the account holder's responsibility to configure.

Who else necessarily sees the data

Data reaches the account holder's own bank or card issuer, which already holds it. Where a third-party open-banking provider is configured to retrieve account data, that provider processes the data in order to supply it — for example, PSD2 Account Information Service providers operating under European regulatory oversight. Such providers are separate controllers or processors and are subject to their own published terms and data-protection obligations.

Encrypted network transport (TLS) is used wherever data leaves the host. Optional overlay networking software may be used to reach the host privately; such networks encrypt traffic peer-to-peer and relay no data to third parties.

Retention

Data is retained indefinitely until the account holder deletes it, because the value of the software depends on a complete historical record. Deletion means deleting the database and the stored import files on the host; encrypted backups the account holder has made are the account holder's responsibility to expire or purge.

Your rights

The account holder has direct and complete access to, and control over, all their data. They can read it, export it, correct it, and delete it at any time without asking anyone. There is no separate copy held by anyone else that would need a request, because there is no intermediary.

Security

Bank access uses read-only, time-limited, revocable consent (PSD2 / Open Banking). Bank credentials are never requested, transmitted, or stored by this software — the account holder authorises directly with their bank. The long-lived cryptographic key used to authenticate to a bank data provider is stored on the host, never in the application database.
